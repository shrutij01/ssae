# Shared context is nuisance; change is the observation

Suppose an activation is generated from latent factors (c) by an unknown map
(q): (z=q(c)). A related observation changes only a subset of those factors,
producing (\tilde z=q(\tilde c)). The observed shift is

\[
\Delta z=\tilde z-z,
\qquad
\Delta c=\tilde c-c.
\]

A raw sparse autoencoder assumes that (c) itself is sparse. SSAE instead
assumes that **the support of (\Delta c) is sparse**. A biography can contain
many simultaneously active properties while a controlled rewrite changes only
one or two.

## What subtraction buys—and what it does not

Subtraction cancels components that are exactly shared in representation
space. It does not guarantee that the remaining vector is generated by only
the intended concept. Prompts may change wording, length, syntax, or other
latent causes. Pair construction therefore defines the observation model.

With locally linear shift geometry,

\[
\Delta z\approx D\Delta c,
\]

so decoder columns can be interpreted as local directions of change. Strong
nonlinear interactions can make a single global column inadequate even when
the pair is semantically controlled.

## Testable consequence

<div class="hypothesis-sheet">
<p><b>Null \(H_0\).</b> Conditioning on a fixed activation budget and decoder
width, training on controlled differences does not improve held-out recovery
of the changed factors or fidelity of (\Delta z) relative to a matched raw
observation model.</p>
<p><b>Alternative \(H_1\).</b> At least one of those pre-specified quantities
improves, with the effect increasing as observation-level nuisance grows while
the changed support remains sparse.</p>
<p><b>Falsifier.</b> The advantage disappears on carefully matched pairs, or
grows when pairs contain more uncontrolled changes—contrary to the sparse
shift explanation.</p>
</div>

The [held-out steering study](../evidence/heldout-steering.md) tests the next
link in this argument: whether train-selected decoder directions move unseen
paired representations in both directions.
